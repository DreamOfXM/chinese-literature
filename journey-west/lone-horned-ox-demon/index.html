Lone-Horned Ox Demon 独角兕大王

He beat the whole pantheon without ever winning a fight: everything thrown at him was picked up by a ring he wore to bed.

Lair
Gold-Hoop Mountain, Gold-Hoop Cave, in deep winter
Treasure
the '金剛琢' — Laojun's ring from crossing the pass; weapons, fire and water cannot touch it
Owner
Taishang Laojun's blue ox, loose because its herd-boy slept

The great deeds 大事記

  1. ch. 50

    The Step Outside the Circle

    畫圈遭擒

    Wukong draws a circle in the road with his club and swears it is stronger than bronze walls; the monk, hungry and cold, steps outside it and the mountain's cave takes him, the pig, the friar and the horse.

  2. ch. 51

    One Ring Takes Six Weapons

    圈子套刀

    Nezha loses six weapons to one cast of the ring, then the thunder-god's hammer, the pagoda king's sword, and Wukong's own club, which next morning stands leaning against the east wall of the cave.

  3. ch. 51

    Fire-Dragons Taken by the Ring

    水火無功

    Li Jing reasons that nothing in the ring can take water or fire; the fire department's fire-dragons and fire-horses are taken, and so is half the Yellow River poured out of a jade bowl.

  4. ch. 52

    The Ring That Took the Sand

    金丹陷足

    The Buddha looks with the wisdom-eye, knows exactly who it is, and refuses to say — a loud monkey would only go squabbling up to Spirit Mountain and blame him. He lends eighteen arhats and eighteen grains of golden cinnabar sand to mire the demon's feet. The ring takes the sand.

  5. ch. 52

    The Yellow Dog-Flea

    黃皮虼蚤

    Wukong goes in at night and finds the ring worn on the demon's bare arm like a beaded bracelet, never removed; he turns into a yellow dog-flea and bites him twice. The ox-man only pulls the ring higher and sleeps.

  6. ch. 52

    The Plantain Fan Takes the Ring

    芭蕉扇

    Laojun comes down from the Heaven of Resentment with his plantain fan: one wave and the ring is caught out of the air, two and the demon's legs go soft under him.

結局 The ending

Laojun blows immortal breath through the ring, pierces the ox's nose, knots his own robe-strap to it and rides the beast home; the narrator stops to say that is where a bull's nose-ring gets its name. Nothing is executed. The club and every stolen weapon come back out of the cave, and the fault is laid, in the text, on a boy who found one pill in the alchemy room and fell asleep.
