Ruyi Zhenxian 如意真仙

A rent-seeker, not a man-eater: he fenced in the one spring in the Women's Kingdom that undoes a pregnancy, and charged pilgrims for a bowl.

Lair
the '聚仙庵' at Jieyang Mountain, renamed from a public spring-cave
Weapon
an '如意鉤子' — a long hooked rod he keeps at the well-mouth
Kin
brother of the Bull Demon King; the feud is over his nephew the Red Child

The great deeds 大事記

  1. ch. 53

    One Bowl for Silk and Wine

    破兒洞改

    The 破兒洞 cave whose water was every woman's remedy in a kingdom of women has been taken over by a Taoist who will give none of it freely: bring silk, wine and fruit, and you may have one bowl.

  2. ch. 53

    Revenge for a Ruined Nephew

    為姪報仇

    He hears the name Tang Sanzang and flies into a rage — his nephew, he says, was ruined by Wukong, and he has nowhere to find him for revenge; the monk who came begging for water is the luck he asked for.

  3. ch. 53

    The Keeper Lured from the Gate

    調虎離山

    Wukong fights him out of the gate and up the hillside so that Sha Wujing can walk in with a bucket, and admits afterwards that the quarrel was only the distraction.

  4. ch. 53

    The Arm Broken at the Well

    打折臂膊

    At the well Sha breaks the Taoist's left arm and stops there, on the grounds that he is in human shape and Sha pities him; Wukong takes the hook, snaps it in two, then in four, and throws it down.

結局 The ending

Nothing is killed at Jieyang Mountain. Wukong's stated reason is legal rather than merciful: the man has broken no law, and killing is not as good as letting go — and there is his elder brother the Bull Demon King to consider. The immortal who fenced in a public spring keeps his life, loses his weapon, and is told that whoever comes next for water must not be hindered.
